\begin{abstract}
We prove a sharp pointwise bound for the stabilization of orbit series under
a surjective quandle homomorphism. The depth of a point in the source is at
most the sum of its depth in its full fibre and the depth of its image in
the base. The proof compares the groups generated by left translations
along the two orbit series over a connected base. It follows that
\(\OS\) is closed under extensions. The disjoint sum of finite dihedral
quandles gives a counterexample for \(\OS_\omega\), answering the second
part of Question~5.7 of Bonatto--Crans--Nasybullov--Whitney.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords}\quad quandle, orbit series, extension,
subnormal subgroup.

\section{Introduction and main results}

Bonatto, Crans, Nasybullov and Whitney \cite{BCNW} introduced the
orbit-series classes \(\OS\) and \(\OS_\omega\). In \(\OS\) every orbit series
stabilizes after finitely many steps. In \(\OS_\omega\) a single finite bound
works for all series in the given quandle. Question~5.7 asks whether each
class is closed under extensions. Under the definition in
\cite[Section~2.2]{BCNW}, an extension tests the quotient and each full
fibre individually.

The April 2020 versions \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10227v1}{v1} and
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10227v2}{v2} of \cite{BCNW} stated the
uniform estimate \(n+m\) for \(\OS_n\) in Lemma~5.5 and asserted extension
closure of both \(\OS\) and \(\OS_\omega\) in Corollary~5.6. The proof
given there treats the trivializing classes \(t\mathcal{OS}_n\), where the
base series reaches a singleton and the corresponding term of the source
lies in one fibre. For an \(\OS_n\) base the terminal term can be an
arbitrary connected quandle, and this step requires a different argument.
Version \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10227v3}{v3} retains the
trivializing case of the lemma and introduces Question~5.7, which also
appears on page~301 of the published paper.

For \(x\) in a quandle \(X\), write \(d_X(x)\) for the first stabilization
step of the orbit series obtained by using \(x\) at every step. We set
\(d_X(x)=\infty\) if the series never stabilizes, and take the depth of a
point in a connected quandle to be zero.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(\pi:Q\twoheadrightarrow B\) be a surjective quandle homomorphism,
let \(x\in Q\), and put \(b=\pi(x)\) and \(F_b=\pi^{-1}(b)\). Then
\[
  d_Q(x)\le d_B(b)+d_{F_b}(x).
\]
The bound is sharp for every pair of finite values on the right.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:closure}
The class \(\OS\) is closed under extensions. If \(B\in\OS_n\)
and every full fibre belongs to \(\OS_m\) for a fixed \(m\), then
\(Q\in\OS_{n+m}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:negative}
The class \(\OS_\omega\) is not closed under extensions. A counterexample
has a trivial base and finite full fibres.
\end{proposition}

\section{Orbit series}\label{sec:prelim}

A \emph{quandle} is a nonempty set \(X\) with an operation \(\conj\)
such that \(x\conj x=x\), each left translation \(L_x(y)=x\conj y\)
is a bijection, and
\(x\conj(y\conj z)=(x\conj y)\conj(x\conj z)\).
A subquandle is closed under the operation and inverse left translations.
The group \(\Inn(X)=\langle L_y:y\in X\rangle\) acts by automorphisms.
We write \(\Orb(x,X)\) for the orbit of \(x\) under this group.
A quandle is connected if the action is transitive and trivial if
\(x\conj y=y\) for all \(x,y\).

An orbit series has \(X_0=X\) and \(X_{i+1}=\Orb(x_i,X_i)\) with
\(x_i\in X_i\). Its stable term is connected. The principal orbit series
at \(x\) has \(P_0^X(x)=X\) and \(P_{i+1}^X(x)=\Orb(x,P_i^X(x))\).
Thus \(d_X(x)=\min\{i\ge0:P_i^X(x)=P_{i+1}^X(x)\}\), with
\(\min\varnothing=\infty\). The class \(\OS_n\) consists of quandles
whose every orbit series stabilizes by step \(n\). In particular,
\(\OS_0\) consists of the connected quandles and
\(\OS_\omega=\bigcup_{n\ge1}\OS_n\).
The class \(t\mathcal{OS}_n\) requires every orbit series to reach a
singleton by step \(n\). Set
\(t\mathcal{OS}_\omega=\bigcup_{n\ge1}t\mathcal{OS}_n\).

For an arbitrary orbit series \(X_i\) and an index \(j\ge0\),
choose \(y\in X_{j+1}\). Its principal series agrees with the given
series through step \(j+1\), since \(y\) belongs to each of the
successive orbits in that prefix. All points of \(X_1\) have the same
principal depth in \(X\), since an inner automorphism of \(X\)
transports their principal series. Therefore, for any \(x\in X_1\),
\(X_j=X_{j+1}\) if and only if \(d_X(x)\le j\). In particular,
membership in \(\OS\) and \(\OS_n\) can be tested on principal series.

A surjective homomorphism \(\pi:Q\twoheadrightarrow B\) induces
a surjection \(\rho:\Inn(Q)\to\Inn(B)\) sending \(L_y\) to \(L_{\pi(y)}\).
Indeed, \(\pi L_y^{\pm1}=L_{\pi(y)}^{\pm1}\pi\), so a word that acts
trivially on \(Q\) induces the identity on \(B\), and the generators
of \(\Inn(B)\) lift. In particular, \(\pi\) maps each orbit onto the orbit
of its image. Iterating this observation on successive restrictions gives
\(\pi(P_i^Q(x))=P_i^B(\pi(x))\) for every \(i\).
Each full fibre \(F_b\) is a subquandle since \(L_y^{\pm1}\) preserves
\(F_b\) for \(y\in F_b\), by \(L_b(b)=b\).

For a subquandle \(S\subseteq Q\), the group
\(G(S)=\langle L_y:y\in S\rangle\) is taken inside \(\Inn(Q)\);
its restriction to \(S\) is \(\Inn(S)\). Equivariance of translations,
\(gL_yg^{-1}=L_{g(y)}\), shows that if \(g\) preserves \(S\),
then it normalizes \(G(S)\).
In particular, if \(x\in S\subseteq T\) are subquandles, then
\(\Orb(x,S)\subseteq\Orb(x,T)\).

\section{Proof of the pointwise bound}\label{sec:quandles}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:connectedbase}
In the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, suppose \(B\) is connected.
Write \(P_i=P_i^Q(x)\), \(F_i=P_i^{F_b}(x)\) and \(G_i=G(P_i)\).
Then \(P_i=G_i\cdot F_i\) for every \(i\ge0\). In particular,
\(d_Q(x)\le d_{F_b}(x)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(U_i=G(F_i)\) and \(K=\rho^{-1}(\operatorname{Stab}_{\Inn(B)}(b))\).
Monotonicity of orbits under inclusion gives \(F_i\subseteq P_i\).
Since \(P_{i+1}=G_i\cdot x\) and \(F_{i+1}=U_i\cdot x\), equivariance
gives \(G_{i+1}\trianglelefteq G_i\) and
\(U_{i+1}\trianglelefteq U_i\). Also
\(U_0\trianglelefteq K\), because \(K\) preserves \(F_b\).

Since \(\rho(G_0)\) acts transitively on \(B\), every point of \(Q\)
can be sent to \(F_b\) by an element of \(G_0\). This proves
\(P_0=G_0\cdot F_0\). Assume \(P_i=G_i\cdot F_i\). Then every
generator \(L_y\) of \(G_i\) is a \(G_i\)-conjugate of a generator of
\(U_i\), so the image of \(U_i\) normally generates \(G_i/G_{i+1}\).

The equality \(\pi(P_{i+1})=B\) gives \(\rho(G_{i+1})=\Inn(B)\).
For every \(g\in G_i\), choose \(h\in G_{i+1}\) with \(\rho(h)=\rho(g)\).
Then \(gh^{-1}\in K\cap G_i\), so \(K\cap G_i\) maps onto \(G_i/G_{i+1}\).
Intersect the chain from \(U_i\) through \(U_0\) to \(K\) with \(G_i\).
Since \(U_i\subseteq G_i\), this makes \(U_i\) subnormal in
\(K\cap G_i\). Its image is therefore subnormal in \(G_i/G_{i+1}\).
A subgroup that is subnormal by a finite chain and normally generates
its ambient group is the whole group. Indeed, the penultimate subgroup
in the chain is normal and contains that normal closure, and the same
reasoning applies successively to the shorter chain. Consequently,
\(G_i=G_{i+1}U_i\), and
\[
  P_{i+1}=G_i\cdot x=G_{i+1}\cdot(U_i\cdot x)=G_{i+1}\cdot F_{i+1}.
\]
This completes the induction. If \(F_m=F_{m+1}\), then
\(F_m=U_m\cdot x\), so \(P_m=G_m\cdot F_m=G_m\cdot x=P_{m+1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the inequality in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
It suffices to consider finite \(r=d_B(b)\) and \(m=d_{F_b}(x)\).
The restriction \(P_r^Q(x)\twoheadrightarrow P_r^B(b)\) has
connected base. Its full fibre \(E=P_r^Q(x)\cap F_b\) over \(b\)
satisfies \(P_r^{F_b}(x)\subseteq E\subseteq F_b\). Iterating the
monotonicity of orbits on the two inclusions gives
\[
  P_{r+i}^{F_b}(x)\subseteq P_i^E(x)\subseteq P_i^{F_b}(x)
  \qquad(i\ge0).
\]
For \(i=m\) the two outer terms coincide and are connected. Thus
\(d_E(x)\le m\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:connectedbase} gives
\(d_{P_r^Q(x)}(x)\le m\). Since the principal series in \(P_r^Q(x)\) is
the tail of the series in \(Q\), this yields \(d_Q(x)\le r+m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:closure}]
If the base and all full fibres belong to \(\OS\), the two depths on
the right of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are finite for every \(x\).
The equivalence of the two tests in Section~\ref{sec:prelim} gives
\(Q\in\OS\). Under the uniform hypotheses the same argument gives
\(d_Q(x)\le n+m\) for all \(x\), hence \(Q\in\OS_{n+m}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness and the counterexample}\label{sec:negative}

Let \(D_{2^k}\) be the quandle on \(\Z/2^k\Z\) with
\(a\conj b=2a-b\), including the one-point quandle \(D_1\).
For \(0\le j\le k\) and \(x\) in a coset
\(S=a+2^j\Z/2^k\Z\), we have
\[
  \Orb(x,S)=x+2^{j+1}\Z/2^k\Z.
\]
The values \(L_y(x)=2y-x\) as \(y\) varies in \(S\) fill this coset,
and each \(L_y^{\pm1}\) preserves it. In particular, every principal
orbit series of \(D_{2^k}\) has depth \(k\).

\begin{proof}[Sharpness in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For \(r,m\ge0\), reduce \(D_{2^{r+m}}\) modulo \(2^r\) to get an
epimorphism onto \(D_{2^r}\). Every fibre is isomorphic to \(D_{2^m}\)
by writing its elements as \(a+2^r t\) in a fixed coset. The depths
of the source, base and fibre at the corresponding points are
\(r+m,r,m\), respectively. BCNW use the same family of epimorphisms
after Corollary~5.20 of \cite{BCNW} to show sharpness for local reductivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:negative}]
Take \(Q=\bigsqcup_{k\ge1}D_{2^k}\) as in \cite[Example~4.2]{BCNW}.
The operation is inherited on each summand and has \(x\conj y=y\)
across different summands. This makes the projection to the index
set a surjective homomorphism onto a trivial quandle in \(\OS_1\).
Its full fibres are the finite summands \(D_{2^k}\), so they belong to
\(\OS_\omega\).

At a point of \(D_{2^k}\), the first orbit in \(Q\) is the same as in
that summand, because the other summands act trivially there.
The subsequent terms are the same as well, and the first step is
strict in both quandles since \(k\ge1\). Thus the depth in \(Q\)
is \(k\), and \(Q\notin\OS_\omega\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same projection also disproves extension closure of
\(t\mathcal{OS}_\omega\) as stated in \cite[Corollary~5.6]{BCNW}.
Each full fibre reaches a singleton in a uniformly bounded number of
steps within that fibre. There is no bound common to all fibres.
Lemma~5.5 of \cite{BCNW} assumes a common bound, so its conclusion for
the trivializing classes is consistent with this example.
\end{remark}
