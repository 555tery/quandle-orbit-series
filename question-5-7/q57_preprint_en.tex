\begin{abstract}
We prove a sharp pointwise bound for surjective quandle homomorphisms.
The stabilization depth of the principal orbit series at a point in the
source is at most the sum of its depth in its full fibre and the depth of
its image in the base. The proof compares the subgroups generated by left
translations along the orbit series of the source and the fibre over a
connected base and uses subnormality of finite defect. The bound implies
that \(\OS\) is closed under extensions. If the base belongs to \(\OS_n\)
and all full fibres belong to \(\OS_m\) for a common \(m\), then the
source belongs to \(\OS_{n+m}\). A disjoint sum of dihedral quandles
shows that \(\OS_\omega\) is not closed under extensions. This answers
both parts of Question~5.7 of Bonatto, Crans, Nasybullov and Whitney.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords}\quad quandle, orbit series, extension,
subnormal subgroup.

\section{Introduction and main results}

Bonatto, Crans, Nasybullov and Whitney~\cite[Section~4.1]{BCNW} studied
classes of quandles defined by stabilization conditions on orbit series.
In \(\OS\), each orbit series stabilizes after finitely many steps.
In \(\OS_\omega\), one finite bound works for all series in a given
quandle. Question~5.7 of~\cite[p.~301]{BCNW} asks whether \(\OS\) and
\(\OS_\omega\) are closed under extensions. Under the definition
in~\cite[Section~2.2]{BCNW}, a class is closed under extensions if, for
every surjective quandle homomorphism, membership of the base and all
full fibres in the class implies membership of the source.

For trivializing orbit series, a quantitative analogue is proved
in~\cite[Lemma~5.5]{BCNW}. If the base belongs to \(\tOS_n\) and each
full fibre belongs to \(\tOS_m\), then the source belongs to
\(\tOS_{n+m}\). The image of the \(n\)-th term of the series is a
singleton, so that term lies in one full fibre and the fibre bound
applies directly. With ordinary stabilization, the image of the
\(n\)-th term can be a connected quandle with several elements. In that
case the term meets several full fibres, so the same argument does not
apply directly. The authors also note that some extensions involving
\(D_3\) and infinite trivial quandles do not decompose as direct
products. They cite this as a reason to doubt a positive answer to
Question~5.7~\cite[p.~302]{BCNW}.

We answer both parts of Question~5.7. Write \(d_Q(x)\) for the first
stabilization index of the principal orbit series of \(Q\) with fixed
anchor \(x\), and set \(d_Q(x)=\infty\) if the series never stabilizes
(see Section~\ref{sec:prelim}). Our main result gives a sharp pointwise
bound using only the depth of the chosen point in its fibre and the
depth of its image in the base.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(\pi\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow B\) be a surjective quandle
homomorphism. Let \(x\in Q\), \(b=\pi(x)\), and \(F_b=\pi^{-1}(b)\).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth}
  d_Q(x)\le d_B(b)+d_{F_b}(x).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for any integers \(r,m\ge 0\), there exist an extension
\(\pi\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow B\) and a point \(x\in Q\) for which,
with \(b=\pi(x)\) and \(F_b=\pi^{-1}(b)\), we have
\(d_B(b)=r\), \(d_{F_b}(x)=m\), and \(d_Q(x)=r+m\).
\end{theorem}
\end{samepage}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:closure}
The class \(\OS\) is closed under extensions. Moreover, if an extension
\(\pi\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow B\) satisfies \(B\in\OS_n\) and
\(\pi^{-1}(b)\in\OS_m\) for all \(b\in B\), with \(n,m\ge 0\), then
\(Q\in\OS_{n+m}\).
\end{corollary}

In particular, \(Q\in\OS_1\) if the base is connected and all full fibres
are trivial, or if the base is trivial and all full fibres are connected.
In each case one of the two depths on the right of~\eqref{eq:depth} is
zero and the other is at most one.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:negative}
The class \(\OS_\omega\) is not closed under extensions. There is a
counterexample with a trivial base and finite full fibres.
\end{proposition}

\section{Orbit series}\label{sec:prelim}

We work with nonempty left quandles. The operation \(\conj\) satisfies
\(x\conj x=x\) and
\(x\conj(y\conj z)=(x\conj y)\conj(x\conj z)\), and each left translation
\(L_x\colon y\mapsto x\conj y\) is bijective. A subquandle is closed
under \(L_x\) and \(L_x^{-1}\) for each of its elements \(x\).
The inner automorphism group \(\Inn(Q)=\langle L_x : x\in Q\rangle\)
acts on \(Q\) by automorphisms, and \(\Orb(x,Q)\) denotes the orbit
of \(x\) under this action. A quandle is \emph{connected} if
\(\Inn(Q)\) acts transitively and \emph{trivial} if \(x\conj y=y\)
for all \(x,y\in Q\).

If \(S\subseteq Q\) is a subquandle, the subgroup
\(\langle L_y : y\in S\rangle\le\Inn(Q)\) preserves \(S\), and its
restriction to \(S\) is \(\Inn(S)\). Hence, for every \(x\in S\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ambient-orbit}
  \Orb(x,S)=\langle L_y : y\in S\rangle\cdot x.
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\Orb(x,S)\) is a subquandle of \(S\), and the map
\(S\mapsto\Orb(x,S)\) is monotone under inclusion among subquandles
containing \(x\). For every automorphism \(g\in\Aut(Q)\) and
every \(y\in Q\), we have the equivariance identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equivariance}
  gL_yg^{-1}=L_{g(y)}.
\end{equation}

An \emph{orbit series} of \(Q\) is obtained by successively choosing
anchors \(x_i\in Q_i\) and setting
\[
  Q_0=Q,\qquad Q_{i+1}=\Orb(x_i,Q_i)\qquad(i\ge 0).
\]
If \(Q_i=Q_{i+1}\), then \(Q_i\) is connected and every subsequent term
equals \(Q_i\). For a fixed \(x\in Q\), define the \emph{principal orbit
series} with anchor \(x\) and the \emph{depth} of \(x\) by
\[
  P_0^Q(x)=Q,\qquad P_{i+1}^Q(x)=\Orb(x,P_i^Q(x)),\qquad
  d_Q(x)=\min\{i\ge 0 : P_i^Q(x)=P_{i+1}^Q(x)\}.
\]
We use \(\min\varnothing=\infty\) and the usual addition on
\(\Z_{\ge 0}\cup\{\infty\}\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:principal}
For any orbit series \((Q_i)_{i\ge 0}\) of \(Q\) and any point
\(x\in Q_1\),
\[
  \min\{i\ge 0 : Q_i=Q_{i+1}\}=d_Q(x).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every automorphism \(g\in\Aut(Q)\) maps the principal series of \(x\)
onto the principal series of \(g(x)\). Since \(Q_1=\Orb(x_0,Q)\) is
an orbit under \(\Inn(Q)\le\Aut(Q)\), any two points in \(Q_1\) are
related by an automorphism of \(Q\), so their principal series have
the same stabilization index.

Fix \(i\ge 0\) and choose \(y\in Q_{i+1}\). For each \(0\le j\le i\),
we have \(y\in Q_{i+1}\subseteq Q_{j+1}=\Orb(x_j,Q_j)\), so
\(\Orb(y,Q_j)=\Orb(x_j,Q_j)=Q_{j+1}\). Induction on \(j\) shows that
the initial segment \(Q_0,\dots,Q_{i+1}\) coincides with
\(P_0^Q(y),\dots,P_{i+1}^Q(y)\). Since \(y,x\in Q_1\), the equality
\(Q_i=Q_{i+1}\) holds if and only if \(P_i^Q(x)=P_{i+1}^Q(x)\).
Taking the minimum over \(i\) gives the result.
\end{proof}

By~\cite[Corollary~4.7]{BCNW}, every orbit series of a quandle in
\(\OS\) is principal. In arbitrary quandles there may be nonprincipal
series with empty intersection~\cite[Example~4.9]{BCNW}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:principal} identifies the stabilization index of any
orbit series with \(d_Q(x)\) for each \(x\in Q_1\), even without
the assumption \(Q\in\OS\).

By Lemma~\ref{lem:principal}, membership in \(\OS\) is equivalent to
\(d_Q(x)\) being finite for every \(x\in Q\), and membership in
\(\OS_n\) (\(n\ge 0\)) is equivalent to \(d_Q(x)\le n\) for every
\(x\in Q\). In~\cite[Section~4.1]{BCNW}, the unions of the classes
\(\OS_n\) and \(\tOS_n\) start at \(n=1\), and connected quandles
belong to \(\OS_1\)~\cite[Example~4.3]{BCNW}. We also allow \(n=0\).
The class \(\OS_0\) consists of connected quandles, and \(\tOS_0\)
consists of one-element quandles. The unions are still
\(\OS_\omega=\bigcup_{n\ge 1}\OS_n\) and
\(\tOS_\omega=\bigcup_{n\ge 1}\tOS_n\).
The class \(\tOS\) consists of quandles in which every orbit series
reaches a singleton in finitely many steps. In \(\tOS_n\), every
such series reaches a singleton by step \(n\).

An \emph{extension} is a surjective quandle homomorphism
\(\pi\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow B\). For each \(b\in B\), the full
fibre \(F_b=\pi^{-1}(b)\) is a subquandle of \(Q\). Indeed,
\(L_b(b)=b\), so \(L_y^{\pm 1}(F_b)=F_b\) for every \(y\in F_b\).

\begin{lemma}[cf.~{\cite[Section~2.2 and Lemma~5.1]{BCNW}}]\label{lem:images}
A surjective quandle homomorphism \(\pi\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow B\)
induces a surjective group homomorphism
\(\sigma\colon\Inn(Q)\twoheadrightarrow\Inn(B)\), given by
\(L_y\mapsto L_{\pi(y)}\), with
\(\pi(g(z))=\sigma(g)(\pi(z))\) for all \(g\in\Inn(Q)\) and \(z\in Q\).
If \(S\subseteq Q\) is a subquandle and \(x\in S\), then
\[
  \pi(\Orb(x,S))=\Orb(\pi(x),\pi(S)).
\]
In particular, the images of the terms of any orbit series form an
orbit series in \(B\) with anchors \(\pi(x_i)\), and
\(\pi(P_i^Q(x))=P_i^B(\pi(x))\) for every \(i\ge 0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(\pi L_y=L_{\pi(y)}\pi\) gives
\(\pi L_y^{-1}=L_{\pi(y)}^{-1}\pi\). If a product of translations
and their inverses acts trivially on \(Q\), then the corresponding
product acts trivially on \(B\) by surjectivity of \(\pi\). Thus
\(\sigma\) is well defined, and it is surjective because each
generator \(L_w\) (\(w\in B\)) lifts to a left translation on \(Q\).
Applying this argument to
\(\pi|_S\colon S\twoheadrightarrow\pi(S)\) gives the equality of
orbits. Apply the equality successively to the terms of the series.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the pointwise bound}\label{sec:quandles}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the bound~\eqref{eq:depth} in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Write \(P_i=P_i^Q(x)\) and \(F_i=P_i^{F_b}(x)\). First suppose that
\(B\) is connected. Consider the following subgroups of \(\Inn(Q)\).
\[
  G_i=\langle L_y : y\in P_i\rangle,\qquad
  U_i=\langle L_y : y\in F_i\rangle,\qquad
  K=\sigma^{-1}\bigl(\Stab_{\Inn(B)}(b)\bigr).
\]
By monotonicity of \(\Orb(x,-)\), the inclusion \(F_0=F_b\subseteq Q=P_0\)
gives \(F_i\subseteq P_i\) for all \(i\ge 0\), so \(U_i\le G_i\).
Also, for every \(y\in F_i\subseteq F_b\), we have
\(\sigma(L_y)=L_b\in\Stab_{\Inn(B)}(b)\). Hence \(U_i\le G_i\cap K\).
By~\eqref{eq:ambient-orbit}, \(P_{i+1}=G_i\cdot x\) and
\(F_{i+1}=U_i\cdot x\). Each orbit is invariant under the corresponding
group, so~\eqref{eq:equivariance} gives \(G_{i+1}\trianglelefteq G_i\)
and \(U_{i+1}\trianglelefteq U_i\). Every \(k\in K\) preserves \(F_b\)
because \(\pi(k(y))=\sigma(k)(b)=b\) for \(y\in F_b\).
Thus~\eqref{eq:equivariance} also gives \(U_0\trianglelefteq K\).
Finally, connectedness of \(B\) and Lemma~\ref{lem:images} give
\(\pi(P_i)=P_i^B(b)=B\) and \(\sigma(G_i)=\Inn(B)\) for all \(i\ge 0\).

We prove by induction on \(i\ge 0\) that \(P_i=G_i\cdot F_i\).
For \(i=0\) and any \(z\in Q\), connectedness of \(B\) and surjectivity
of \(\sigma\colon G_0\twoheadrightarrow\Inn(B)\) give an element
\(g\in G_0\) with \(\pi(g(z))=b\). Thus \(g(z)\in F_0\) and
\(z\in G_0\cdot F_0\).

Suppose \(P_i=G_i\cdot F_i\) for some \(i\ge 0\). For every
\(y\in P_i\), we can write \(y=g(z)\) with \(g\in G_i\) and \(z\in F_i\).
By~\eqref{eq:equivariance}, the translation \(L_y=gL_zg^{-1}\) is
conjugate in \(G_i\) to an element of \(U_i\). Therefore \(U_i\) normally
generates \(G_i\), and its image normally generates \(G_i/G_{i+1}\).

The restriction of the canonical projection
\(G_i\twoheadrightarrow G_i/G_{i+1}\) to \(G_i\cap K\) is surjective.
Indeed, for every \(g\in G_i\) the equality
\(\sigma(G_{i+1})=\Inn(B)\) gives \(h\in G_{i+1}\) with
\(\sigma(h)=\sigma(g)\). Then \(\sigma(gh^{-1})=\mathrm{id}_B\), so
\(gh^{-1}\in G_i\cap K\) and \((gh^{-1})G_{i+1}=gG_{i+1}\).
Intersecting the finite subnormal chain
\[
  U_i\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq U_0\trianglelefteq K
\]
with \(G_i\cap K\) shows that \(U_i\) is subnormal of finite defect in
\(G_i\cap K\). By surjectivity of \(G_i\cap K\twoheadrightarrow G_i/G_{i+1}\),
its image \(U_iG_{i+1}/G_{i+1}\) is subnormal of finite defect in
\(G_i/G_{i+1}\). A subnormal subgroup of finite defect that normally
generates a group equals the whole group. Otherwise, the last proper
subgroup in the subnormal chain would be a proper normal subgroup
containing the normal closure. Hence \(G_i=G_{i+1}U_i\), and
\[
  P_{i+1}=G_i\cdot x=(G_{i+1}U_i)\cdot x
  =G_{i+1}\cdot(U_i\cdot x)=G_{i+1}\cdot F_{i+1}.
\]
This completes the induction.

If \(m=d_{F_b}(x)<\infty\), then \(F_m=F_{m+1}=U_m\cdot x\).
Since \(U_m\le G_m\), we have
\[
  P_m=G_m\cdot F_m=G_m\cdot(U_m\cdot x)=(G_mU_m)\cdot x
  =G_m\cdot x=P_{m+1}.
\]
Thus \(d_Q(x)\le m\) when \(B\) is connected.

Now let \(B\) be arbitrary. It suffices to consider the case where
\(r=d_B(b)\) and \(m=d_{F_b}(x)\) are finite. By Lemma~\ref{lem:images},
the restriction of \(\pi\) gives a surjection
\(P_r^Q(x)\twoheadrightarrow P_r^B(b)\), whose base \(P_r^B(b)\)
is connected by the definition of \(r\). The full fibre over \(b\)
is \(E=P_r^Q(x)\cap F_b\). Monotonicity of orbits gives
\(F_r\subseteq P_r^Q(x)\), and \(F_r\subseteq F_b\), so
\(F_r\subseteq E\subseteq F_b\). Iterating monotonicity yields
\[
  F_{r+i}\subseteq P_i^E(x)\subseteq F_i\qquad(i\ge 0).
\]
For \(i=m\) and \(i=m+1\), the two outer terms equal \(F_m\), since
\(r+i\ge i\ge m\). Thus \(P_m^E(x)=F_m=P_{m+1}^E(x)\), giving
\(d_E(x)\le m\). Applying the connected-base case to
\(P_r^Q(x)\twoheadrightarrow P_r^B(b)\) gives
\(d_{P_r^Q(x)}(x)\le m\). The principal series of \(P_r^Q(x)\) is
the tail of the principal series of \(Q\) starting at step \(r\),
so \(d_Q(x)\le r+m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:closure}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:principal}, \(Q\in\OS\) if and only if \(d_Q(x)\)
is finite for all \(x\in Q\), and \(Q\in\OS_{n+m}\) if and only if
\(d_Q(x)\le n+m\) for all \(x\in Q\). Applying~\eqref{eq:depth} to each
\(x\in Q\) gives both assertions under their respective hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness and the counterexample}\label{sec:negative}

For an integer \(k\ge 0\), set \(A_k=\Z/2^k\Z\), and let \(D_{2^k}\)
be the dihedral left quandle on \(A_k\) with operation \(a\conj b=2a-b\).
For \(k=0\), this is the one-element quandle \(D_1\). For every coset
\(S=a+2^i A_k\) and every \(x\in S\), we have
\[
  \Orb(x,S)=x+2^{i+1}A_k.
\]
Indeed, \(S=x+2^i A_k\). As \(y=x+2^i u\) ranges over \(S\), the
elements \(L_y(x)=2y-x=x+2^{i+1}u\) fill the coset \(x+2^{i+1}A_k\).
For every \(y\in S\) and \(z\in x+2^{i+1}A_k\), we have
\(L_y(z)=2y-z\in x+2^{i+1}A_k\). Since \(L_y^{-1}=L_y\), this
coset is invariant under \(\Inn(S)\). Thus the principal series
of \(D_{2^k}\) at \(x\) consists of the cosets \(x+2^i A_k\)
(\(0\le i\le k\)) and stabilizes exactly at step \(k\), where it
reaches a singleton subquandle. These depths were also computed
in~\cite[Example~4.1]{BCNW}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of sharpness in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For any integers \(r,m\ge 0\), reduction modulo \(2^r\) defines a
surjective quandle homomorphism
\[
  D_{2^{r+m}}\twoheadrightarrow D_{2^r}.
\]
The full fibre over \(0\) is \(2^r A_{r+m}\) and is isomorphic to
\(D_{2^m}\) via \(u\bmod 2^m\mapsto 2^r u\bmod 2^{r+m}\).
At \(0\), the depths in the source, base and fibre are \(r+m\), \(r\)
and \(m\), respectively. The same family of extensions is used after
Corollary~5.20 of~\cite{BCNW} to show sharpness of the bound on the order
of local reductivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:negative}]
Take the disjoint sum from~\cite[Example~4.2]{BCNW},
\[
  Q=\bigsqcup_{k\ge 1}D_{2^k}.
\]
The operation is inherited within each summand, and \(u\conj v=v\)
for elements from different summands.
Checking the cases according to which summands the elements belong to
shows that \(Q\) is a left quandle. The projection
\(\pi\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow B\) that sends the elements of
\(D_{2^k}\) to \(k\) is a surjective homomorphism onto the trivial
quandle \(B=\Z_{\ge 1}\). The base \(B\) belongs to \(\OS_1\), and each
full fibre \(D_{2^k}\) is finite and belongs to \(\OS_k\subseteq\OS_\omega\).

Since translations by elements of other summands act trivially on
\(D_{2^k}\), for any point \(x\in D_{2^k}\) the terms of the principal
series in \(Q\) agree with those in \(D_{2^k}\) from the first step onward.
Thus \(d_Q(x)=k\) for all \(k\ge 1\), including \(k=1\), when
\(P_0^Q(x)=Q\) is infinite and \(|P_1^Q(x)|=1\). The depths in \(Q\)
are therefore unbounded, and Lemma~\ref{lem:principal} gives
\(Q\notin\OS_\omega\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In the extension from the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:negative},
the base belongs to \(\tOS_1\) and each full fibre \(D_{2^k}\) belongs
to \(\tOS_k\subseteq\tOS_\omega\). Every orbit series in the source
\(Q\) reaches a singleton subquandle, so \(Q\in\tOS\), but there is
no uniform finite bound on the number of steps and hence
\(Q\notin\tOS_\omega\). This illustrates why a common upper bound
\(m\) for all full fibres is essential when applying
Lemma~5.5 of~\cite{BCNW} to \(\tOS_\omega\) (cf.\ Corollary~5.6
of~\cite{BCNW}). Under the definition of closure used
in~\cite[Section~2.2]{BCNW}, both \(\tOS_\omega\) and \(\OS_\omega\)
fail to be closed under extensions.
\end{remark}
